Wedding Photobooth Ideas Guests Will Love

By PhotoboothOnline · Updated October 17, 2026 · 3 min read

A good photobooth is one of the few wedding extras that every age group uses. These wedding photobooth ideas help you choose the style, place it well and turn the strips into keepsakes.

Why A Photobooth Works At Weddings

Weddings mix family, friends and colleagues who may not know each other. A photobooth gives them a shared activity and an easy way to talk. It also produces candid pictures that professional wedding photographers rarely capture, because guests relax when no one with a big camera is watching. The strips become an informal album of everyone who attended.

Choose The Right Style Of Booth

For a formal wedding, a mirror booth or an open-air booth with an elegant backdrop looks polished. For a rustic or relaxed celebration, a classic enclosed booth or a DIY setup with fairy lights fits better. If your guest list is large, choose open-air so groups can fit; if it is small, a curtained booth feels intimate. Our overview of types of photobooths compares them in detail, and the rental guide lists what to ask providers.

Pick A Smart Location

Place the booth where guests naturally pass, such as near the bar, the entrance to the reception or the lounge area, but not so close to the dance floor that music drowns conversation. Make sure there is enough space for a queue, a power outlet and a good light source. A booth hidden in a corridor will be ignored, while one placed beside the cocktail hour will be busy all night.

Design The Backdrop And Props

Match the backdrop to your colour palette: a floral wall, a velvet curtain, a sequin panel, a plain paper roll or a wall of greenery all work. Choose props that suit the tone, such as signs with in-jokes, oversized glasses, crowns, hats and speech bubbles. Avoid cheap, flimsy props that break by the end of the first hour. For more inspiration, read photobooth backdrop ideas and photobooth props ideas.

Personalise The Strip

Add the couple’s names and date as a caption or overlay so that every strip is a souvenir. With the caption field in our free photobooth, you can type a short message such as “Sam & Alex · 12 June”. Choose a frame colour that matches your invitations, and a black-and-white or vintage filter for a timeless look.

Turn Strips Into Keepsakes

  • Guest book: ask each guest to stick a strip on a page and write a message beside it.
  • Favours: give guests a copy to take home.
  • Display: pin strips on a string with mini pegs for a decorative wall.
  • Album: collect digital copies into a shared folder for the couple.

Timing And Etiquette

Open the booth right after the ceremony so guests can use it during the cocktail hour, and keep it running through the evening. Place a short instruction sign to speed up the queue, set a countdown that gives guests enough time to pose, and appoint a friend to tidy the props every hour. If you want to avoid distractions during speeches, ask the operator to pause the booth.

Budget-Friendly Options

If a rental is outside your budget, a laptop or tablet running an online photobooth, a simple backdrop and a lamp can deliver much of the fun. Place a small card explaining how to use it, and designate a friend to look after the setup. See our DIY photobooth guide for the step-by-step process.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where should a wedding photobooth be placed?

Near the bar, the lounge or the entrance to the reception, where guests pass naturally, with space for a queue and a power outlet.

How many guests can use a photobooth at a wedding?

It depends on the booth and the length of each session. Open-air booths with quick sessions can serve large guest lists, while a single enclosed booth suits smaller groups.

Is a photobooth worth it for a small wedding?

Yes, especially a simple or DIY one. It gives guests a keepsake and a reason to mingle, even with a small guest list.
